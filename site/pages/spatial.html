This page is about the part of FASP that carries that disagreement honestly instead of hiding it. facts: - [Invariant, No value without uncertainty] - [Time, An interval, not a number] - [Pose, A mean and a covariance] - [Separation, Decided at a stated risk] ---

The invariant. No value crosses a machine boundary without its uncertainty attached. A timestamp is an interval, not a number. A pose is a mean and a covariance. A frame relationship carries a drift rate and decays. A separation verdict quotes the residual risk it was decided at.

This is not conservatism for its own sake. It is the only way a decision made from stale, remote, imprecise data can state what it is actually worth — and the only way two machines with no shared clock and no shared map can still agree about who may move.

Implementation fasp_harness/spatial/

Why the bound is worse than the number. A four-timestamp exchange reduced to its core, plus two things a naive implementation omits — and both omissions make the estimate look better than the link actually is.

Min-filtered, not averaged

Samples are min-filtered per time bucket. On a shared radio the round trip is dominated by queueing, so the least-queued sample is the tightest bound available — while averaging drags the estimate into the tail. The test recovers 40 ppm of drift through 200 ms spikes on a 20 ms floor precisely because of this.

Drift is never assumed to be zero

Until skew is actually measurable, the reported bound falls back to the datasheet worst case. That looks worse than a tight optimistic number, and it is correct: 50 ppm is 180 ms of error per hour, and nobody wants to discover that at hour three.

Nothing in this module returns a bare offset. θ is exact only if both path directions took equal time, and on a real radio they did not.

fasp_harness/spatial/clock.py

A frame link is a claim that decays. The relationship between two coordinate frames was true when it was measured. Publishing it without its age would be publishing a number that is quietly wrong.

  • Kabsch / Umeyama alignment with the determinant correction, so a reflection cannot masquerade as a rotation.
  • Covariance fitted from the residual, not assumed — the quality of the fit becomes part of the answer.
  • Composition through the SE(3) adjoint, so chained links compose correctly instead of accumulating error twice.
  • Links decay. A visual alignment ten minutes old reports metres of position sigma rather than the millimetres it had when fresh.
  • Collinear correspondences are refused, not fitted. Rotation about that line is unobservable, and Kabsch will happily return a number for it anyway.

fasp_harness/spatial/frames.py

Nothing acts on a position it received. It acts on the position that position will have reached by now, with a covariance that grew on the way.

Timing uncertainty becomes position uncertainty at v · ε. Two hundred milliseconds of clock slop at 1.5 m/s is thirty centimetres of position error, no matter how good the sensor was.

Process noise is per-domain, and the difference is not cosmetic. A ground vehicle is anisotropic: heading error multiplied by distance driven is cross-track error, not radial error. A rotorcraft is isotropic and roughly forty times noisier, because gusts dominate its motion model. Using one covariance for both produces a verdict that is wrong in two different ways at once.

fasp_harness/spatial/state.py

The guard band is the number, and the reservation uses the same one. Not two numbers that drift apart — a nasty failure, because each half looks correct in isolation.

  • An axis-aligned box, not a sphere. k·√(Pii) is the exact support of the k-sigma ellipsoid along axis i. A sphere would over-protect the axes you care about to cover the ones you do not.
  • Sized as max(statistical, reachable) — never a sum, which would double-count the motion since the report.
  • Reachability is per-axis from the motion model, because a wheeled robot cannot climb at its ground speed.
  • Conflict is a predicate over morphology pairs. Air and subsurface are separated by the water column whatever their coordinates say, while a drone landing on a ground robot falls through to ordinary 3D geometry with no altitude special-casing.

spatial/reservation.py is deliberately the seam where the guard band and the reservation are the same computation. Duplicating that arithmetic would be a future disagreement waiting for a bad day.

A slower radio is a wider guard band is a wider aisle

fasp guard-budget answers the question an integrator asks once the radio is chosen and before the aisle width is fixed. Feed it the p99 round trip, not the mean — queueing on a shared radio is heavy-tailed, and the mean describes a link nobody experiences.

fasp_harness/spatial/guard.py · CLI gate runs in CI

Spatial authority is an ordinary grant with extra constraints. That is the whole trick — it inherits signature, revocation and audit unchanged, because it is not a parallel mechanism.

  • Bounded in space, time and speed. Not "you may proceed" — a volume, a window and a rate.
  • Symmetric between platforms. Holder and subject are names, not roles, so the logic does not care which machine is nominally in charge.
  • Carried in the grant's constraints. The existing grant machinery validates it, expires it, revokes it and audits it.
  • It expires without a message. There is no revocation to lose when the link drops — silence is the safe direction.
  • Silence quietly shrinks it. The envelope grows as the link ages, until it no longer fits inside the delegated volume.

fasp_harness/spatial/authority.py

Exact overlap is right for a ledger and wrong for robots. Two machines that disagree about the time, and do not know precisely where they are, cannot be told apart by comparing millisecond ranges and cell names.

Reservation segments can therefore carry two optional additions:

guard_ms

Clock doubt, declared

The requester's clock uncertainty plus its decision margin. Comparison is guard-to-guard, so two reservations ten milliseconds apart on paper correctly conflict when their owners' clocks disagree by two hundred. A deployment with good clocks gets its tight packing back.

volume

Geometry, not a cell name

An axis-aligned box in a named frame, already dilated by the guard band. A cell name is a convention two vendors must agree on first; a box in a named frame is not — so two robots sharing no cell vocabulary still cannot occupy the same air.

A rejection reports which test caught it, because "your cell map is wrong" and "your geometry overlaps" are different problems with different fixes. Both additions are capped: thirty seconds of declared clock doubt is a clock fault to fix, not a larger reservation to grant.

Adding covariances correctly is the part everyone gets wrong. P_a + P_b is only valid when the two share nothing at all.

Frame links routinely do share things — a common anchor set, the same SLAM session. Under such sharing the cross-covariance is unknown, so the default bound is the one that holds for any cross-covariance: sigmas add linearly rather than in quadrature.

The cost is √2 for two equal hops. Pass correlated=False where independence can actually be justified — and justify it, rather than assuming it because the number is nicer.

Why no numeric dependency

All of the spatial maths is pure Python. Adding a numeric library would mean more wheels in an audited edge install, and every wheel is a supply-chain question that has to be answered again on every appliance.
